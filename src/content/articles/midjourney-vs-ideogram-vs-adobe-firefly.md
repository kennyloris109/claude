Any AI image generator can now make a decent picture from a prompt. These three are worth comparing because each leads on a different thing: Midjourney on how the images look, Ideogram on getting text right, and Adobe Firefly on what you're allowed to do with the result.

## How we compared them

We compared them on the jobs people buy image tools for: editorial and hero images, social graphics with headlines, product mockups, a consistent character or style across a series, and editing existing photos. We also weighed free-plan access, API availability and commercial-use terms.

## Midjourney: best for looks

Midjourney's lighting, composition and texture still look the most deliberate of any generator. Designers use it when the image is the main thing on the page.

Style references and moodboards keep a look consistent across dozens of images, which helps a blog or brand. The web editor handles inpainting, outpainting and retexturing, so you no longer need Discord, and you can animate a still image into a short clip.

There's no free plan and no public API, so you can't plug Midjourney into an automated publishing pipeline the way you can with the other two.

## Ideogram: best for text in images

Ideogram gets the words right. Ask for a poster that says "Summer Sale: 40% off" and you get those exact words, set legibly, which few generators manage reliably.

That makes it the one to use for YouTube thumbnails, Pinterest pins, social posts, event posters and logo ideas. Magic Prompt turns a short idea into a detailed prompt, and an API lets you generate graphics from code.

Its photographic and artistic images are very good, but a step behind Midjourney's best.

## Adobe Firefly: best for commercial work

Firefly is most useful inside Photoshop (Generative Fill and Generative Expand), Illustrator and Adobe Express. Adobe designed it for commercial use, which matters to agencies and brands.

Generative Fill in Photoshop is still one of the most useful AI features for editing real photos. On some plans the Firefly app also gives you partner models from other vendors, and businesses that need it can get IP indemnity on enterprise plans.

Images made straight from a prompt can look more like stock photos than Midjourney's, and the credit system across Adobe's plans takes some reading.

## Quick decision guide

| You need… | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A striking hero image or concept art | Midjourney |
| A graphic with a headline, price or logo | Ideogram |
| To edit or extend a real photo | Adobe Firefly (in Photoshop) |
| Images generated automatically by code | Ideogram or Firefly (both have APIs) |
| Client work with legal review | Adobe Firefly |

## The bottom line

Use [Midjourney](/tools/midjourney/) when the image has to look great, [Ideogram](/tools/ideogram/) when it has to carry words, and [Adobe Firefly](/tools/adobe-firefly/) for commercial work and Photoshop editing. If you only need an image now and then, the generator built into [ChatGPT or Gemini](/compare/chatgpt-vs-claude-vs-gemini/) may be enough without another subscription.
